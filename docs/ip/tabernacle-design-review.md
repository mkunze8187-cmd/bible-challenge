# Agon — Tabernacle Checkpoint IP Review Notes

**Purpose:** Technical disclosure inventory for a qualified IP attorney or freedom-to-operate reviewer; **not** a patentability, novelty, copyright-clearance or noninfringement opinion. **Status:** design approved, not necessarily implemented. Source of truth: [Tabernacle checkpoint specification](../../specs/games/exodus-tabernacle.md), [Exodus Journey #461](https://github.com/mkunze8187-cmd/bible-challenge/issues/461), [cooperative work #578](https://github.com/mkunze8187-cmd/bible-challenge/issues/578).

## What is being designed
A Scripture-grounded cooperative Tabernacle construction experience for 1–4 teams, with an independently explorable blueprint, one shared worksite, asynchronous parallel production and installation, shared warehouse, short material-request challenges and longer construction challenges, activity handoff/pause, dependency-controlled installation, section-specific learning inspections, clickable final study review, and a simple canonical Exodus 40 presentation.

## Technical features to assess as combinations (not assertions of invention)
1. **Hidden quantitative logistics with visible feasibility:** authoritative exact warehouse/activity ledgers but nonnumerical player-facing projections, including hidden transfer and consumption quantities, material-type indicators, availability/insufficiency feedback, limited no-quantity supplemental requests, and non-leaking returns/Stage/controllers.
2. **Challenge-gated cooperative production pipeline:** Scripture/content challenges authorize initial and supplemental withdrawals, batch/single-piece manufacture, and permanent step-level installation; failures are non-destructive; concurrent activity ownership and safe idempotent commits.
3. **Seeded bounded recipe variability:** game-resource requirements vary modestly across sessions/batches without changing biblical specifications; saved seeds and content versions reproduce outcomes, and end-of-game accounting does not enable a guaranteed future formula.
4. **Persistent parallel worksite with voluntary handoff:** no permanent team specialization, paused tasks retain allocations and progress, teams can help/resume/release surplus, with dependencies tracked per step and progressive common visual assembly.
5. **Education layered on construction:** one reusable, variably detailed Scripture blueprint; unscored locked initial estimates; section-level discrepancy-finding inspections that do not alter authoritative completed construction; final interactive canonical review.

These may be ordinary, previously known, or obvious game/software patterns individually or in combination. Counsel should assess specific claims and prior art; do not use this list as evidence of novelty.

## What should not be claimed as proprietary
- Biblical passages, the canonical Tabernacle's dimensions, materials, furniture, symbolism explicitly in Scripture, the events of Exodus 40, or facts in the public domain.
- Generic inventory, crafting, resource management, educational questions, hidden information, seeded randomization, construction visualization or cooperative multiplayer in isolation.
- Third-party Bible translations, illustrations, music, UI artwork or text without verifying license/ownership. Keep translation pack entitlements separate.

## Materials for legal review / evidence to retain
- Versioned specs, decision history, PRs, issues, diagrams of authoritative runtime vs Stage/controller projections, material-request/production/installation state machines, seeded variation/replay test cases, and dated design/implementation records.
- Inventory of externally sourced art, sound, fonts, code dependencies, question content, translation licenses and contractor contributions; record provenance and applicable permissions.
- Prior-art search focused on cooperative crafting games, challenge-gated inventory systems, hidden-resource accounting, recipe variation and multiplayer task handoff; examine patent **claims**, not just product similarities.
- Separate copyright/trademark, patent novelty/nonobviousness and patent freedom-to-operate assessments. Distribution method and jurisdictions remain undecided; revisit risk/cost when those are decided.

## Proposed review questions
- Are any *specific technical combinations* plausibly protectable, and what evidence/diagrams or prototype is needed before filing?
- Could any implemented feature practice claims of active patents, regardless of independent creation?
- Which assets, licensed translations and third-party dependencies require clearance before public distribution?
- Does publishing a public repository or disclosing the design affect filing timelines in target jurisdictions? Seek jurisdiction-specific counsel **before** relying on any grace period.
