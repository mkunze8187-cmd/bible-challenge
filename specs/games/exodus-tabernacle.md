# Exodus Journey — Tabernacle Construction Checkpoint

**Status:** Approved design; implementation pending. **Parent:** [Exodus Journey #461](https://github.com/mkunze8187-cmd/bible-challenge/issues/461). **Related reuse:** [cooperative work #578](https://github.com/mkunze8187-cmd/bible-challenge/issues/578), [Bible Builders #334](https://github.com/mkunze8187-cmd/bible-challenge/issues/334), [Kids Tabernacle Blueprint #249](https://github.com/mkunze8187-cmd/bible-challenge/issues/249). This is an Exodus-specific composition of reusable worksite, inventory, challenge and inspection capabilities; it does not redefine those other games.

## Canonical and learning boundaries
- Exodus 25–31 and 35–40 govern the Tabernacle design, materials, dimensions, arrangement, craftsmanship, offerings and conclusion. All playthroughs yield the **complete, biblically faithful Tabernacle**. Players cannot alter canonical events or the final design.
- Individual Bible challenge difficulty follows each player's profile; game difficulty controls guidance/operational assistance, not Bible-content difficulty. No AI/ML at runtime; all content authored/versioned, with reproducible seeded variation.
- The worksite and learning material are data-driven and pack-compatible under Agon-owned schema contracts. Packs configure content, not executable engines. Bible passages and explicit facts must never be obscured to manufacture a hidden-game-resource puzzle.

## Intro: Study the Pattern / Prepare the Worksite
- Interactive, layered, clickable blueprint: whole layout, courtyard, framework, coverings, Holy Place, Most Holy Place, furnishings, garments and component details where applicable.
- Element entries are **variable-detail**: identify the element and its Scripture references; include description, construction, materials, dimensions, placement, purpose, significance, what it represents, and OT/NT connections **only where applicable and supportable**. Omit empty/invented categories. Clearly label explicit Scripture, supported cross-Scripture connections and interpretive symbolism.
- Blueprint is reusable as a standalone study resource and remains freely available to individuals throughout the checkpoint, without pausing other teams.
- Teams collaboratively explore and form **one initial estimate and construction plan** (materials and dependencies). This is instruction/planning, unscored, not a test or start gate. Finalized initial plan is locked, viewable as reference, and cannot be collaboratively revised during play. Actual authoritative dependencies and consumption are independent of guesses. Compare plan with results at the end.

## Worksite, team coordination and warehouse
- 1–4 player-controlled teams, up to four players each, may operate in parallel; twelve tribes may be represented narratively and as background contributors. No tribe has intrinsic specialty or bonus. One authoritative shared worksite progressively displays installed parts.
- Agon coordinates, but does not compel, activity choice: available work, dependencies, bottlenecks, shortages, assistance requests and guidance scaled by game difficulty. Essential operational truth is always available.
- Any team can pause **any** activity at any time, return it to the shared queue, switch tasks, or voluntarily join/help where supported. Another team resumes exact authoritative progress. Activity-owned materials, findings and partial work persist; no permanent team ownership. An activity can be resumed solely to release unused materials.
- Central warehouse stores raw inputs, intermediate and finished components. Transfers are immediate, not a transport game. Materials belong to activities, never teams. A paused activity keeps its allocation. Unused materials may be released; incorporated/installed material cannot be reclaimed.
- Background **Gather the Offerings** is narrative and supply delivery, **not a gameplay activity** (Exodus 35:20–29; 36:3–7). Warehouse begins partly stocked and receives intermittent, seeded, nonuniform contributions by material and milestone. Temporary shortages are possible; contributions eventually exceed actual requirements and include the narrative of Moses stopping offerings because more than enough was supplied. Do not deliver exactly-on-demand rescue supplies; ensure no permanent deadlock.

## Reusable activity pipeline
- **Production:** take inputs from the warehouse; perform component-appropriate, longer Scripture-based challenges; on success consume internally tracked inputs, produce one piece (e.g., Ark) or a batch, and deposit finished outputs in the warehouse. Production, preparation and installation can proceed concurrently.
- **Installation:** take finished components and any needed supporting supplies; check **step-level** structural dependencies, not blanket activity locks; perform longer placement/assembly/purpose-oriented Scripture challenges; on success consume inputs and permanently add the completed part/section to the shared visual Tabernacle. Different teams can install distinct sections concurrently, including before every component of a larger area is produced. Installed components never return to the warehouse.
- Preparation is eligible in parallel; dependencies govern when prepared components can be installed. Challenge types may include matching, sequencing, Scripture interpretation, locating/placing elements and relating purpose/significance, not only trivia.
- **Every initial material request and every supplemental request** requires a shorter Scripture challenge tied to the requested material or component. Check warehouse feasibility **before** challenging. For an initial request, players choose the desired quantity based on their estimate; if insufficient stock, they may accept available supply or pause. Subsequent requests use **Request More** with no player-entered quantity: internally limited transfer amounts, potentially requiring multiple successful challenges. The supplemental limit belongs to the activity, not a particular team, and is configurable by material/activity. Do not auto-supply the exact shortfall.
- Before each production piece/batch or installation step, internally check that allocated inputs suffice. If not, say **insufficient**, offer to request more or pause; do **not** disclose quantity required for that batch, total recipe or shortfall. Do not present an impossible production challenge. Wrong challenge answers transfer/consume/produce/install **nothing**, never waste materials or undo completed substeps; retries can use another suitable challenge. Successful challenges commit atomically/idempotently.
- Exact accounting is authoritative **internally** but **hidden from all player-facing views during construction**: no warehouse total or per-material numerical stock; no actual transfer amount even for a known initial request; no activity-held quantity, per-batch consumption, remaining recipe quantity or exact shortfall. Activity UI indicates material types held and supply sufficient/insufficient; warehouse indicates available/insufficient/unavailable or can provide. Do not leak values through before/after changes, release confirmations, projections, event logs, controller/Stage/API responses or repeated request feedback. The initial player-entered estimate/request remains known to its authors, but is not proof of quantity delivered. Returning unused materials does not reveal quantities.
- Hidden game-resource consumption uses **modest, bounded, reproducible seeded variation across playthroughs and optionally batches** (e.g. cutting/fitting efficiency). Fixed biblical quantities, materials, dimensions, component count and final arrangement NEVER vary. A past game's accounting must not yield a guaranteed future recipe. Variation should reward thoughtful estimates, not random guessing; exact bounds/tuning remain for implementation playtesting, not approved numeric percentages. Persist seed, content versions and authoritative resource ledger for save/resume and audit.

## Inspection and completion
- Inspections are **section-by-section application of learning**, available when a meaningful installation section is complete, not a prerequisite for that section's completed state. Two tribes/teams participate cooperatively (use game-managed tribe for solo player-controlled team). Other work can continue. The scene may include out-of-place, extraneous or incorrectly placed objects, or be entirely correct. Teams inspect, identify discrepancies or confirm correctness, then receive Scripture-grounded explanation.
- Inspections **never change construction done state**, never require repairs or additional work, and never consume resources. A maximum of **one or two surprise inspections across the checkpoint**, not per section/team, use the same learning-only behavior; avoid clustering/disrupting all work.
- After all construction and section reviews, present **one interactive, unscored final review** of the complete **correct** Tabernacle. Click/tap items to access the same variable-detail, Scripture-grounded blueprint content. This is exploration, not another scored inspection.
- Close with a **simple animation or narrated slideshow** of Exodus 40:34–38: cloud covering the Tent of Meeting, glory of the LORD filling it, Moses unable to enter, and cloud/fire guiding Israel. No additional challenges, choices, scoring or complex cinematic engine. Canonical narrative and checkpoint completion are assured.

## Final accounting and reflection
- **Only after completion**, show shared cooperative accounting: contributions received, initial estimates/requests, actual quantities delivered, supplemental requests, material consumption, unused returns, remaining surplus and completed outputs. Distinguish received vs used; never imply excess requests are requirements. No individual performance penalty/score. Historical actuals can be shown but do not reveal seed-generation formula or future recipe.

## Reuse and implementation boundaries
- Reuse #578's authoritative concurrent pipeline patterns where suitable, but its Bricks Without Straw oppressive-scoring rules do not transfer. Generic capabilities: shared worksite tasks and handoff, dependency graph, hidden-inventory projections, challenge-gated transfers and production/installation, seeded recipe ledger, multi-tribe visual inspection, clickable authored study model. Exodus owns Tabernacle content, narrative, presentation and configuration.
- Runtime owns truth; controller intents and Stage/public projections must not expose hidden ledger data. Test concurrent withdrawals/production, partial stock, repeated requests, failure with zero consumption, pausing/reassignment, non-leaking views, seeded replay, no deadlocks, all 1–4 team configurations, and Scripture-fixed completed state.
