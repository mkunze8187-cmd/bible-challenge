# Chapter 3 — Passover & Deliverance — Passover Elements / Cooperative Puzzle Reveal (gameplay locked 2026-10-04)
**Scripture frame: Ex 12:1–32. Classification: COMPOSE_EXISTING for current design -> Challenge Resolver + #579 Composite Cooperative + shared voting/communications/input/persistence contracts. Reassess extraction only if the opaque-barrier/reveal presentation or mixed-puzzle assembly gains additional consumers.**

## Experience intent
This checkpoint is deliberately **one-party cooperative**. Teams retain distinct identities/controllers and receive Player-Profile-appropriate Bible Challenges, but nobody wins the checkpoint over another team. The party earns, deduces, assembles and experiences the final reveal together.

The gameplay explores Scripture/topics related to **elements present in the Passover event** without reducing the checkpoint to Exodus 12 trivia. Challenges may draw Bible-wide material semantically related to selected elements (for example lamb/sheep/shepherds/sacrifice, blood, households, bread/leaven, firstborn, judgment, protection, readiness, deliverance, slavery/freedom, remembrance, obedience or redemption).

Before the finale, authored Challenge/content selection MUST avoid explicitly giving away the intended Christ/Passover-Lamb reveal. Jesus/the Cross is not a puzzle answer, shortcut, buzzer answer or scored discovery.

## Hidden Passover Elements
At session generation, select a configurable set of approximately **4–5 Passover Elements** from a larger authored pool.

The selected Elements are **never announced to players during gameplay**. Players must infer what Elements are present from:
1. the thematic pattern across the Scripture Challenges they encounter; and
2. the visual/structural evidence in the puzzle pieces they earn.

Challenges are associated semantically with the hidden selected Elements, but the **puzzle piece attached to a Challenge is randomized across all selected puzzles**. A sheep/shepherd Challenge therefore does not necessarily award a piece from the Lamb puzzle. This prevents Challenge topic from becoming a direct piece-sorting label.

The completed puzzle may automatically identify/name its Element after completion; players do not need to type a redundant label such as "lamb" versus "sheep."

## Phase 1 — Challenges / private piece collection
All selected Elements' Challenges are mixed into one earning phase rather than completing one Element at a time.

- Challenges are assigned across represented teams/players using normal Player Profile/audience/controller resolution.
- **Bible Challenge difficulty always comes from Player Profile**, never Passover puzzle/game difficulty.
- Each required Challenge opportunity carries a randomly assigned required puzzle piece.
- Correct answer -> earn that piece.
- Incorrect answer -> no piece loss/penalty; resolve **another equivalent profile-appropriate Challenge** until the required piece is earned.
- Every required piece must remain obtainable; a missed Challenge can never make puzzle completion impossible.
- Earned pieces remain **private to the team that earned them** during collection.
- Other teams cannot inspect that team's private collection.
- Pieces are not assembled during the Challenge phase.
- The shared Stage may indicate overall collection/progression without exposing private piece faces or their puzzle identity.

The Challenge is the gate that earns the piece; its answer does not need to describe the particular piece awarded.

## Phase 2 — Mixed cooperative assembly
After all required pieces are earned, transition into one shared assembly phase containing all **4–5 hidden puzzles at once**.

Players are not told which puzzle represents which Element. They must solve two coupled problems:
1. **which puzzle does each piece belong to?**
2. **where does it fit within that puzzle?**

Pieces use irregular/interlocking cuts. The puzzles should be similar enough in presentation/cutting grammar that they cannot be trivially sorted by border style, piece geometry or obviously different visual treatment.

### Turn structure
Teams alternate authoritative assembly moves. On its turn a team chooses exactly one assembly action:
- **PLACE:** take one piece from its still-private collection and attempt to place it on a chosen puzzle board; or
- **MOVE:** reposition a piece that has already been exposed/placed on the shared board.

Once a private piece is committed/exposed to a puzzle board, it becomes **public/shared working information**. It may be discussed and may be moved on later turns regardless of which team originally earned it.

A mistaken puzzle classification is therefore recoverable through later MOVE actions.

## No correctness oracle
Assembly MUST NOT tell players that a piece is assigned to the wrong puzzle or wrong general area.

Do not provide:
- "wrong puzzle" messages;
- red/green correctness highlighting;
- destination hints;
- automatic puzzle classification;
- neighbor completion hints;
- other semantic correctness leakage.

The only placement feedback is **physical/structural fit**: if the irregular piece fits the attempted spot, it can be placed; if it does not fit that spot, it cannot be placed there.

## Cooperative assistance / communications
Before committing one of its private pieces, the active team may request the existing **all-team advisory vote/recommendation**.

That vote answers **only which puzzle the other teams recommend for the piece**. It does not vote on the exact position within that puzzle, and the active team retains the final placement decision.

Once the puzzle choice is made and the piece is exposed on the shared board, all teams can see it and may use the normal allowed communication/chat channels for open cooperative discussion about exact placement, orientation, relationships to other pieces, or whether already-public pieces should later be moved.

This preserves a meaningful information boundary:
**private earned piece -> optional which-puzzle vote -> commit/expose -> public cooperative discussion.**

## Puzzle/game difficulty
Passover **game difficulty changes the deduction/assembly problem, never Bible-question difficulty**.

Difficulty may tune:
- number of hidden puzzles (target approximately 4–5);
- pieces per puzzle / total piece count;
- irregular-cut complexity;
- similarity of the puzzles' art/backgrounds/textures/palettes;
- amount of distinctive imagery visible on individual pieces;
- similarity/ambiguity of visual regions across different puzzles;
- whether pieces require rotation where supported;
- puzzle-art variant selection.

Illustrative progression:
- **Easy:** more visually distinct puzzle imagery/backgrounds; sorting still requires deduction but provides stronger visual help.
- **Medium:** more unified treatment and overlapping visual characteristics.
- **Hard:** strongly similar art/background treatment with intentionally ambiguous piece-level cues.
- **Expert:** may use **pictures composed of words/text** associated with the hidden Element. Individual pieces may contain only word/letter fragments or visually similar typography; the recognizable image/Element emerges primarily through assembly.

All puzzles in one session use compatible cutting grammar/comparable geometry so shape-generation style itself does not reveal puzzle membership.

Content Packs may provide multiple art/puzzle variants per Element. Session randomization should combine selected Elements, art variants, cuts/layouts, Challenge selection, Challenge-to-piece binding, team assignment, assembly arrangement and reveal presentation to create high replayability.

## Automatic puzzle completion
When all pieces of one hidden puzzle are correctly assembled, completion is recognized **automatically**. Do not require a redundant typed/selected Element-name answer.

The completed puzzle may then identify/name the discovered Passover Element and advances one shared reveal-mechanism milestone.

## Opaque reveal mechanism / progress
The checkpoint uses an **opaque barrier/reveal mechanism** whose visible state is the party's progress indicator. Examples include a vault/safe, closed castle doors, curtain, sealed scroll, stone wall, dark doorway or other authored compatible presentation.

The mechanism remains completely opaque throughout gameplay. **Nothing behind it is partially revealed.**

Each completed Element puzzle changes one mechanism-specific progress state, for example:
- retract a locking bolt;
- release a chain/bar/brace;
- break an outer seal;
- release a curtain clasp;
- remove/weaken a support;
- illuminate a surrounding lamp.

Progress must be communicated through the mechanism itself rather than a generic "4 of 5 tasks" treatment where practical.

The final Element completion triggers a substantially distinct **atomic opening/reveal**. The barrier does not crack open progressively and does not expose portions of the final image during earlier milestones.

## Christ / Passover reveal
After the final puzzle is complete, competitive gameplay ends and the mechanism executes the authored **Cross / Jesus-as-Passover-Lamb reveal**, followed by the Passover/Gospel narrative connection.

The Cross/Christ connection is a **reveal and narrative conclusion**, not a player-solved answer. Appropriate authored Scripture may connect Exodus 12 to later biblical revelation (for example John 1:29, 1 Cor 5:7, 1 Pet 1:18–19), with exact narration/text finalized in content authoring.

The reveal remains on screen for a configurable minimum **X-minute hold**. Timer expiry enables Continue; it MUST NOT auto-advance. Host advances after the minimum hold.

During the reveal/narrative:
- no buzzer or quiz;
- no scoring event;
- no team winner treatment;
- no team-specific completion credit;
- team standings/colors/competitive UI should be hidden or visually de-emphasized so the Stage becomes a shared presentation surface.

## Shared scoring invariant
**Every represented team receives exactly the same configured checkpoint score.**

No differential points or penalties are awarded for:
- correct/incorrect Challenge attempts;
- number of Challenges answered;
- which pieces a team earned;
- placing or moving pieces;
- following/overriding a puzzle recommendation vote;
- identifying a puzzle's theme;
- completing an Element puzzle;
- making the final placement;
- the Cross reveal or narrative.

The checkpoint result is a **shared party value awarded equally to every team**, regardless of individual contribution distribution. This is intentional: the experience should communicate that the party reached the reveal together, not that one team reached it first.

## Canonical / theological guardrails
- Gameplay explores elements/themes associated with the first Passover but does not introduce later formal Seder practices into the Exodus event.
- The canonical Passover includes the commanded lamb, blood, unleavened bread/bitter herbs and associated obedience/remembrance as established by the selected translation/content.
- Challenge semantic association must not turn topical similarity into an unsupported theological assertion.
- The tenth plague/death of the firstborn MUST NOT become a competitive scoring target or playful casualty mechanic.
- Player success does not cause or prevent God's canonical judgment/deliverance.
- Christ as Passover Lamb is presented through authored biblical narrative/reveal, not inferred as a required puzzle answer.

# Chapter 3 — Passover Reveal, Tenth Plague, and Departure Transitions (locked 2026-10-05)

## Cross reveal is an interpretive pause, not a chronological time jump
The atomic Cross / Jesus-as-Passover-Lamb reveal at the end of the Passover Elements checkpoint is an **interpretive/theological pause outside the Exodus Journey chronology**.

It MUST NOT be presented as though the represented Israelites literally travel forward to the time of Jesus and then return to Egypt. Instead, the Journey temporarily steps outside the historical scene so the players can understand the later biblical fulfillment/connection that the preceding gameplay intentionally withheld.

During this pause:
- the Exodus environment may visually recede or disappear so the Cross/reveal has its own shared presentation space;
- authored narration/Scripture may explicitly make the Christ/Passover-Lamb connection;
- the Cross remains revelation/narrative, never a puzzle answer or scored discovery;
- no competitive/team winner UI intrudes on the shared reveal.

## Return from the Cross reveal to Passover night
After the configured minimum reveal hold and Host chooses Continue, transition **back into the historical Passover night**.

A preferred visual grammar is for the Cross to remain visible briefly while imagery from the first Passover gradually returns around it—households, doorway/blood, lamb, unleavened bread/readiness and other appropriate Exodus 12 imagery—then allow the Cross to recede/fade until the historical scene alone remains.

The transition/narration should make the chronology clear: the players have been shown the later fulfillment, but the Journey is now returning to what Israel was experiencing that night in Egypt.

This connection MUST NOT imply that the Israelites at that moment possessed the later New Testament revelation just presented to the players.

## Passover night / Tenth Plague — narrative-only checkpoint
**No gameplay is required or permitted for the Tenth Plague sequence.** This is an intentional narrative-only Journey checkpoint under the #458 rule that authored narrative does not need filler gameplay.

Present the Passover night and Ex 12:29–32 faithfully and solemnly:
- households have followed the canonical Passover instructions;
- the blood/protection state is canonical narrative, not something caused by puzzle performance;
- players do not decide who is protected;
- there are no Challenges, choices, interactive objects, targets, points, penalties, success/failure states or competitive actions during the plague;
- the death of the firstborn is never converted into a casualty counter, target, scoring opportunity or playful mechanic;
- presentation should be restrained/non-graphic; darkness, stillness, environment, narration, sound and the grief described by Scripture can carry the gravity without depicting individual deaths graphically;
- Pharaoh summoning Moses and Aaron and commanding Israel to leave remains a fixed canonical event.

The emotional/presentation rhythm should intentionally slow after the Cross reveal: **understanding -> return to Passover night -> stillness -> judgment/grief -> Pharaoh's command to leave.**

## Handoff into Goshen Escape Room
Pharaoh's command to leave is the point where player agency returns.

Transition from the narrative Tenth Plague/Pharaoh sequence into the already-defined **Goshen Escape Room**. The Escape Room does not cause Pharaoh to release Israel and is not a prison-break claim; Pharaoh has already told Israel to go.

The intended rhythm is:
**Passover cooperative gameplay -> atomic Cross reveal -> interpretive pause -> return to historical Passover -> narrative Tenth Plague -> Pharaoh says leave -> Goshen Escape Room.**

This creates a deliberate contrast: player agency is removed for the solemn canonical judgment/release and restored when Israel is told to depart.

## Goshen Escape Room completion -> gathering Israel
Completing the Goshen Escape Room does **not** itself depict the whole nation leaving Egypt. It completes the party's active Goshen search/preparation experience and then hands control back to authored narrative.

After Escape completion:
1. transition out of gameplay;
2. show Israelites **gathering/converging** in and from Goshen;
3. show families/households, possessions, livestock and other canonically appropriate departure details;
4. allow locations explored during the Goshen activity to visually empty/converge into the larger movement where practical;
5. pull the presentation outward enough to remind players that their represented party is participating in the story of the **whole people of Israel**, not that four teams alone constitute the Exodus.

No additional gathering/departure game is required. There is no new scoring event here.

## Leaving Egypt — narrative Journey milestone
Israel's actual departure from Egypt is an authored **narrative/cinematic Journey milestone**, not another gameplay activity.

The departure MUST receive enough visual/narrative time and weight to feel like the culmination of the Journey from the opening slavery/brickyard world through Moses' return, Pharaoh, the plagues and Passover.

Do **not** rush directly from Escape completion to the Red Sea crisis. Players should have time to experience the fact that Israel has finally left Egypt before the next threat develops.

This sequence may show the gathered people beginning to move away from Egypt and establish the scale/relief/urgency of departure while preserving canonical chronology. It should also provide a meaningful visual contrast with the Journey's opening oppression/brickyard imagery and later support the finale's backward retrospective through departure/Goshen to Egypt.

# Chapter 3 — Passover & Deliverance — The Exodus / Goshen Escape Room — Ex 12:33–42 (gameplay locked 2026-10-03)
**Classification: NEW_REUSABLE_CAPABILITY consumer -> Escape Room #600 + Interactive Location Scene #599 + Challenge Resolver + Challenge Assistance #595 + shared persistence/input contracts.**

## Scripture frame / guardrails
Pharaoh has already told Israel to leave. Goshen is therefore an **escape room as a gameplay format**, not a prison-break claim: the party searches/navigates Goshen, follows cryptic clues, gathers needed objects and finds the exit/departure path. Presentation MUST NOT imply Israel was imprisoned in Goshen or that player puzzle success causes Pharaoh's canonical decision.

This intentionally continues the Chapter 2 Darkness experience: Darkness thematically returned represented Israelites to Goshen; this checkpoint begins from Goshen and now asks the party to prepare/find its way out.

## Shared Goshen
One shared party state: one current Goshen location, one current clue, one generated Escape solution, one shared collected-object inventory and one clue history. Discoveries belong to the party, not the team that clicked.

The authored Goshen pool should support roughly **10–20 plausible locations** and roughly **20–30 object types** as initial content targets, while the session selects only a configured subset. Exact counts are content/configuration/playtest values, not engine constants.

Potential locations include homes, lanes, market/trade areas, livestock areas, storage/work areas, well, workshop and gathering areas. The final authored list is Content Pack data.

There are deliberately more object types present in a generated game than object types ultimately collected. Objects may have multiple variants/instances distinguished by visible attributes such as color, shape, size/material/pattern/condition/markings/contents. A market may therefore contain several different baskets or jars. Multiple instances of the same object type can appear in one or several locations.

Goshen uses **location selection**, not Board/Track movement, dice or movement points. Choosing a location moves the whole party directly into that #599 Interactive Location Scene. Puzzle difficulty is figuring out **where to search**, not spending turns walking between spaces.

## Escape packages / generation
Author approximately **3–4 Escape Packages/templates** initially, with compatible authored clue/module variation and constrained randomized location/object-instance assignment where coherent.

At session initialization #600 generates the complete valid solution, validates it, locks it and persists it. Required collectible instances and clue-bearing objects are chosen from compatible generated instances; filler/duplicate/near-match objects make the environment credible and prevent memorized object-type shortcuts.

## Search interaction
Inside a location, all interactable objects are shown/selectable (with an accessible list equivalent). Players do not select a separate clue versus object mode; they inspect an object.

Only the authoritative current clue destination can reveal progress. A future clue-bearing object remains ordinary until its clue is active. Inspecting something early does not exhaust it. Wrong inspection means only 'nothing useful right now' and does not reveal future relevance.

## Dual-stream clues
A typical clue is about **4–5 lines** (content/configurable). Most lines cryptically lead to the **next clue**. A separate line/fragment contributes to an accumulating secondary solution that identifies **both the location and the specific object instance that must eventually be collected**.

Across successive clues, emphasized/bold words or equivalent accessible treatment may combine into that location+object solution. The object may be distinguished from same-type alternatives by generated attributes. The clue chain's next-clue path may travel through locations unrelated to the eventual collectible location.

Players may act on the accumulated collection solution as soon as they believe they have deduced it; do not force them to consume a predetermined number of clues first.

A collectible is not a separate mechanic. Finding the correct collectible instance uses the same discovery flow as finding a clue and then adds it to shared inventory before revealing the next clue.

## Stage state
The **current clue remains visible on Stage until the next clue is successfully found/revealed**, including during votes, choices, wrong searches and Scripture Challenge gates. When progress succeeds, the new clue replaces it. Prior clues remain available in clue history; collected objects remain visible/accessible as shared inventory.

## Cooperative decision control
Every search decision uses **all-team advisory vote -> rotating team final choice**.

Location and object selection are separate actions. All teams vote; after votes commit/reveal, the team holding chooser authority makes the final decision and may follow or override the vote. Final authority rotates so one team does not make consecutive search decisions. A representative four-team sequence is A chooses location -> B chooses object -> C chooses next location -> D chooses next object -> repeat. Stay Here is a valid location action when appropriate.

Voting is not timed/scored for speed. Solo/low-team/controller variants follow semantic represented-actor/input topology.

## All-team Scripture gate
Correctly finding the current clue/object does not immediately reveal it. **All teams simultaneously receive profile-appropriate Bible Challenges.** All represented teams must clear before the clue is revealed or collectible finalized.

Challenge difficulty ALWAYS comes from Player Profile under the Journey-wide invariant; Escape difficulty never changes Bible-question difficulty. A missed Challenge does not make the party re-find the object. #595 assistance/replacement prevents deadlock.

If the party needs an additional **Escape puzzle hint**, it can earn one by all teams clearing another set of profile-appropriate Bible Challenges. Hint count/strength is configurable by game difficulty/package. This increases Scripture engagement without making Bible-question difficulty a puzzle-difficulty lever.

## Wrong searches / scoring
Default: an ordinary wrong location/object choice has **no direct score penalty**; it consumes the search action and chooser rotation advances. A wrong choice that follows the popular/plurality vote likewise has no team-specific penalty.

A chooser who overrides the popular/plurality vote may receive a configurable small team-specific **risk penalty when wrong** and **insight bonus when correct**. Ties are not overrides. Exact values/formulas and any optional shared completion/efficiency/early-deduction scoring remain configurable for playtesting.

No response-speed scoring. Votes themselves do not score. There is no separate wrong-collection penalty because collectible discovery is the same interaction as clue discovery.

## Difficulty
Game difficulty configures the puzzle/environment, including number of selected locations, number/density of object instances/types, number of required collectibles, clue count/depth/crypticity, distractors/near-matches/duplicates, and available hint count/strength. Exact ranges are configurable/playtest data.

## Exit / completion
The **last clue identifies the exit**. Finding/selecting the correct exit after the generated chain requirements are satisfied completes the Escape. Transition immediately back to the canonical Exodus departure narrative. The puzzle does not rewrite Ex 12:33–42.
