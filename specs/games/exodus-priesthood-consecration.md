# Exodus Journey — Consecration of the Priesthood

**Status:** Approved design, implementation pending. **Parent:** [Exodus Journey #461](https://github.com/mkunze8187-cmd/bible-challenge/issues/461). **Scripture:** Exodus 28–29; Leviticus 8–10; supporting offering requirements Leviticus 22:17–25. One cooperative Chapter 4 closing checkpoint; followed by Be Ye Holy and Preparation for Departure. No timer, competition, speed score or fabricated offerings.

## Roles, assignment, authority and controllers
- Players occupy **positions**, not the identities of Moses, Aaron, Nadab, Abihu or other named historical figures. One High Priest position; other priest positions limited by historically supported duties; any additional support roles require separate scriptural substantiation, not invented priestly authority. Game performs unfilled duties. Respect 1–4 teams, 1–4 players/team and individual profile-appropriate content.
- Assign positions by descending role hierarchy. At each level, unassigned players express interest; oversubscription resolved by seeded casting of lots; unsuccessful applicants remain eligible for lower levels. Lots does not rank spiritual worth.
- Before play the shared Stage explains **every** position, authorized actions, restrictions and consequences. Choices are neutrally worded; do not label traps or warn during a turn.
- One active ceremonial turn at a time on the **shared Stage**. Assigned players in the same position rotate **round-robin**. Other connected players wait, unable to submit actions. Distinguish waiting, disconnected (reconnectable, assignment retained), host-deactivated (host may reactivate), and game-deactivated (permanent for checkpoint, host cannot override). Disconnection is not misconduct; game temporarily takes over. Host-deactivated/game-deactivated responsibilities transfer to game.
- Ordinary errors: game corrects **that turn only**, explains error and proper procedure, player remains eligible. Only an explicitly pre-instructed **serious biblical authority violation** permanently game-deactivates a player. Never enact an invalid ceremony action. Inspection judgments, garment errors and votes are ordinary mistakes, not authority violations.
- Stage shows actions, explanations and corrections to all; controller submits authorized intents; runtime is authoritative. Group related actions into meaningful turns rather than micro-clicks. Scriptural order governs, without asserting that historical actions could never overlap.

## Preparation and garments — Leviticus 8:3–13
- Assemble and instruct the congregation; Moses washes Aaron and sons (8:6). Before the visual washing, every controller independently chooses among illustrated washing methods; aggregate choices on Stage, then explain Scripture and plausible historical methods and depict washing. This is a thought exercise, **not** a binding vote. Explicit Scripture is authoritative; historically plausible details Scripture leaves open may have multiple valid choices; avoid anachronisms.
- **High Priest first:** player assembles garments **piece by piece**, from seeded varying subsets of a larger illustrated library. Player may revise selections before submitting full outfit. No correctness feedback until complete. Everyone—including dresser—inspects outfit and privately flags suspected incorrect individual pieces or “no errors”; reveal aggregate votes **before** explanations; then correct only actual errors. Multiple scripturally compliant styles accepted, with unspecified details treated as reconstruction rather than commandments. High Priest must be properly dressed **before anointing**.
- Moses anoints Tabernacle and Aaron (8:10–12) as narrated shared Stage action; no player direction of Moses.
- **Ordinary priests next:** each assigned player assembles their outfit piece by piece with differing valid and invalid garment options. **All** ordinary priest outfits are finished and displayed together **before** anyone's garment correctness is revealed. All players inspect and privately vote on every outfit including their own; show vote distribution, then explain and correct. No votes determine truth or disqualify a player. Every assortment has at least one valid option per required component, but not every valid option is present; avoid identical sets when sufficient content exists. Correct garments can look different. Leviticus 8:7–13 and Exodus 28/39 govern distinct high-priest/ordinary-priest requirements.
- The sequence is high priest clothing and approval → Moses anointing → ordinary priest clothing and group approval, preserving Leviticus 8 chronology.

## Consecration offerings — Leviticus 8:14–30
- One unified ceremony with three distinct prescribed offerings in order: **bullock (young male cattle) for sin** (8:14–17), **ram for burnt offering** (8:18–21), **second ram for consecration** (8:22–29). For each, authorized player chooses animal from varying illustrated distractors (species, sex, age when relevant); **exactly one correct** offering selection. Cow is not interchangeable with bullock. Explain and substitute correct animal on mistake, no disqualification.
- Aaron and sons participate in laying hands on each offering's head, through assigned positions; game fills unassigned roles. Moses carries out the sacrificial actions Scripture assigns to him. No extra invented priestly choices.
- Moses applies consecration blood to **right ear, right thumb and right big toe** (8:23–24): **observe only**, no location-selection quiz. Remaining wave offering, prescribed portions and oil/blood sprinkling on priests and garments (8:25–30) are narrated/visualized, without extra decisions.
- The meal at the entrance and seven-day observance (8:31–36) are narrated without challenges; transition to the eighth day.

## Eighth-day priestly service — Leviticus 9
- Aaron now performs prescribed priestly sacrificial duties; Moses instructs, Aaron's sons assist according to text. High Priest/ordinary priest positions act only within their authorized duties; game fills gaps.
- Prescribed offerings: Aaron's young calf sin offering and ram burnt offering (9:2,8–14); people's male goat sin offering, calf and lamb burnt offerings, ox and ram peace offerings, and grain offering (9:3–4,15–21). Do not invent extra animals or force an arbitrary procession duration.
- **Offering inspection at presentation, before admission for sacrifice:** Israelites bring varying apparently valid/invalid animals or other offerings; priests inspect the offering and determine accept/reject. Priests make final authorization; supporting Levites, if separately scripturally justified, may only observe/assist, never assume priestly authority.
- Evidence includes visible traits (species, sex, age, lameness, blindness, blemish, injury, suitability for offering) and limited question responses. Each encounter allows **2–5 questions**, tuned to complexity and game difficulty; choose from an authored pool with varying phrasing. Some questions decisive, some partial, some unhelpful. Congregants' authored personalities/knowledge yield varied truthful, mistaken, evasive or deliberately false answers; two need not answer identically. Contradictions between testimony and observation are possible. **No dynamic confrontation, defensive behavior or changing stories.** A material unresolved contradiction can prevent acceptance pending verification, but is not itself a biblical animal defect or proof of intentional lying.
- Players can decide before exhausting questions. **Reject requires at least one selected reason**, optionally multiple; include “no errors” only where appropriate to garment inspection, not rejection. Reveal correct outcome and missed defects on Stage. Wrong acceptance is stopped **before sacrifice**; wrong rejection is corrected; neither is game-deactivation. Distinguish eligibility facts from congregant motives. Every encounter is reasonably resolvable with available evidence and questions, not a forced guess. Inspection is **one complete rotating turn** for the position, not a new rotation system.
- The offering procession concludes naturally when all prescribed offerings and priestly duties are complete, without timer or “last offering” announcement. Aaron blesses people, Moses and Aaron enter and emerge, God's glory appears and divine fire consumes offering (9:22–24), all as shared narrated Stage sequence.

## Leviticus 10 closing
- Nadab and Abihu's unauthorized fire and God's judgment are **solemn canonical narrative**, never a player-triggered failure, playable choice or fictional alternate outcome. Explain holiness, obedience and priestly responsibility; close with brief Christian reflection (e.g., 1 Peter 2:9; Romans 12:4–8; 1 Corinthians 12), **without** claiming church members inherit Levitical office.

## Shared deterministic capabilities and content
- Reuse or extend generic illustrated selection/assembly with delayed whole-ensemble validation, group private inspection/voting and public aggregate reveal; seeded variation and category-specific correctness; no fixed single illustration when Scripture permits variety.
- Reusable **evidence-based offering inspection**: versioned authored scenario ground truth, visual evidence, congregant knowledge/intent and authored response variants, question pool/budget, accepted eligibility constraints, required rejection reasons, deterministic seeded encounters, authoritative admission guard and explanation. Avoid AI/ML.
- Generic role assignment by lots, role-scoped authority, round-robin turn scheduler, fallback ownership and deactivation states should be reused rather than reimplemented in this checkpoint. Stage/public and controller/private projections must respect permissions. Authoring/data packs may add compatible content, not schemas or executable mechanics; use Agon-owned versioned schemas and compatibility gating.
- Earlier “exactly one correct action” applies to animal identity and explicit prescribed ceremonial actions; **not** unspecified historical garment/washing appearances, where multiple choices may be acceptable. Vary distractors and their order; never silently classify an ambiguous option as a serious authority violation.

## Acceptance and verification targets
- Scripture order, specified offering inventories and Moses/Aaron duty transition verified against Exodus 28–29, Leviticus 8–10 and applicable offering rules; narrative cannot be altered by players.
- Simulate 1–16 players across supported team configurations, absent roles, disconnect/reconnect, host deactivation, game deactivation, turn rotation and controller authorization.
- Verify high-priest correction precedes anointing; ordinary priest review delayed until all dressed; private votes then public aggregate then Scripture-based correction; varied valid garment options.
- Verify each prescribed offering, hand-laying and observational-only blood application; inspection never admits invalid offerings; rejection reasons required; question limits and scenario solvability; truthful/mistaken/evasive/deceptive response variants, reproducible seeds and no dynamic confrontation.
- No invented offering, timers, scoring, AI, unsupported Levite authority or change to biblical outcomes. Preserve replay and save/resume behavior via authoritative events/content versions.
