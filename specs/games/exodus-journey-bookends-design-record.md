# Journey Bookends — Opening and Finale (locked 2026-10-04)

## Opening cinematic / prologue — Slavery to Burning Bush
The Exodus Journey MUST intentionally establish the visual world that the finale will revisit.

### Opening sequence
- Begin in **Egypt under Israelite slavery/oppression**, with the brickmaking/brickyard environment as a major visual anchor: mud, straw, brick pits/work, oppressive labor and the surrounding Egyptian setting.
- Establish the Journey's starting condition through authored cinematic/narrative presentation rather than making suffering itself a scoring/gameplay mechanic.
- The opening should establish memorable environmental compositions/details that can be recognized when the finale later returns to the same visual world.
- The prologue then carries the canonical story forward through the already-planned material needed to reach Moses in Midian: Israel's oppression, Moses' preservation/upbringing and flight from Egypt.
- The opening MUST **lead naturally into the planned Burning Bush narrative/checkpoint (Ex 3:1–4:17)**. It is not a standalone trailer or a second unrelated introduction.
- Exact narration, shot list, duration, audio/music and final transitions remain authored-content/design work to be finalized later.

### Bookend requirement
The opening and ending are deliberately paired. The brickyard/slavery imagery introduced here is the destination of the finale's retrospective camera movement. Content/presentation authoring should therefore preserve enough stable visual identity for the return to be unmistakable.

## Final Journey sequence — Jordan, Promised Land and retrospective
The complete Exodus Journey ends with an authored finale sequence after the final gameplay. This is the Journey ending, not merely an ordinary checkpoint transition.

### Jordan / Promised Land
1. Final gameplay completes with the **Jordan River still visibly blocking the way**.
2. Completion triggers an **atomic parting of the Jordan**. The river does not gradually open as a gameplay progress indicator.
3. Israel begins crossing through the divided Jordan in the background.
4. As the crossing proceeds, the **Promised Land is progressively revealed**. The gradual reveal belongs to the cinematic traversal after the atomic river-parting event, not to gameplay.
5. The concluding narrative is presented in the foreground while the crossing/environmental reveal continues in the background.
6. The crossing into the land occurs under **Joshua**, preserving the biblical transition from Moses rather than depicting Moses crossing the Jordan.
7. Exact final narrative wording is intentionally deferred for later design.

### Retrospective camera journey
After/while establishing arrival in the Promised Land, the viewpoint rises into a sweeping aerial/drone-like perspective and travels **backward through the Journey**.

The retrospective should visually revisit recognizable environments/events the party experienced, moving generally backward through:
**Promised Land / Jordan -> wilderness -> Sinai/testing/provision locations -> Red Sea -> departure/Goshen -> Egypt -> slavery/brickyard.**

This is not merely a generic highlight reel. Where practical, locations should contain visual echoes/details from the actual Journey so players recognize what God carried Israel through. The exact locations, pacing and narrative synchronization remain to be authored later.

### Final shot — return to the beginning
The retrospective ultimately returns to the **same slavery/brickyard visual world established by the Journey opening**.

The camera descends toward the mud/brickmaking scene. The locked closing visual is:
- straw is thrown toward the camera/mud pit;
- it strikes/splashes into the mud;
- **mud and straw splash onto/cover the screen**;
- that visual closes the Journey.

Final title treatment, fade/hold timing, music/audio and exact narration remain open for later design.

### Finale intent / invariants
- The ending emphasizes not only where Israel arrived, but **what God carried His people through and brought them out of**.
- Jordan parting is atomic; Promised Land reveal during the crossing is progressive/cinematic.
- Finale traversal is presentation/narrative, not additional scored gameplay.
- The retrospective runs backward through the Journey and ends at its beginning, creating an intentional visual bookend.
- The finale MUST NOT rewrite canonical events or imply Moses crosses the Jordan.
- Opening and finale assets/content should be authored together where necessary to preserve visual continuity.


## Exodus chapter transitions / episodic mini-intros
Exodus consumes the Journey chapter-intro contract from #458.

Each Exodus chapter should have a short, consistently styled **episodic mini-intro**—similar in function to the opening of a TV episode—that identifies/reorients the chapter and leads naturally into its first checkpoint.

- Use the same recognizable Exodus chapter-transition grammar across all chapters while allowing chapter-specific visuals, location, Scripture context and narration.
- During uninterrupted play, the mini-intro marks the transition into the next chapter.
- When a saved Exodus Journey resumes at the **start of a chapter**, the same mini-intro provides enough context to reorient players who may be returning after time away.
- The mini-intro is not a full recap and does not replay completed gameplay.
- **Chapter 1's mini-intro is distinct from the full Exodus Journey opening.** A brand-new Journey receives the locked slavery/brickyard prologue that leads into the Burning Bush. Resuming at Chapter 1's start must not automatically replay that complete cinematic; it uses the shorter chapter-entry treatment.
- Exact chapter-by-chapter narration, imagery, duration, music/audio and recap content will be authored later.
